## PART 0 — The big idea, the two epochs, and the path

**The core thesis:** early-stage selling is **evangelical selling** = product marketing + product management + sales, fused, done by a **founder**, with a tight loop between what prospects say and what we build and message. You cannot "sprinkle a sales rep on the product" pre-PMF. A sales pro takes a *known-good* product with a *known-good* message to market repeatably; they are not there to discover the message, the ICP, or the pain. Hiring that person too early is the single most common way startups delay or miss PMF. ("An org can't scale until it fires its first VP Sales" is really a failure of founders who handed a nascent product to someone who wasn't a product manager.)

**Two epochs — know which you're in (we are Epoch 1):**

| | Epoch 1 — Experimentation (pre-PMF) | Epoch 2 — Scaling (post-PMF) |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Is the pain real, how big, what will they pay? | Known-good solution + message; how many humans can sell it? |
| "Sales" is | Founder-led, high-touch, deliberately unscalable, info-gathering | Reps, role specialization, instrumented metrics, inbound machine |
| Do | Manual prospecting, on-site demos even for small deals, hand-built collateral | SDR/AE/CS pods, content marketing, sales ops |

Doing unscalable things now (manual lead-gen copy/paste, on-site visits for <$1k deals, hand implementation) is fine — it's an **information-gathering investment**, not just revenue.

**The full path (the "maturity model" — detail in Part 10):**
`Founder does it all → Founder + SDR (or + CSM) → Founder + SDR + 2 AEs → Initial "Sales Pod" (founder steps out of selling) → Pod abstraction & scale → Full team-of-teams`. Each stage has a **goal** and **exit criteria**, and you move up like easing into a jacuzzi, validating as you go. **When to scale:** when your win rate is reliably **15–30%** at a healthy ACV and deal cycle. Above 30%, raise pricing AND start scaling; below ~15%, fix messaging/product/pricing first.

**Two anti-patterns bracket the path:** *premature scaling* (hire many reps before proving founder-led sale works → burn cash, reps sell to anyone with a pulse, churn later — see Zenefits, Square Stand 2014) and *lagged scaling* (founder keeps "turning the crank" on deals instead of packaging the motion → opportunity cost, competitors gain).

## PART 1 — Sales mindset shifts (Ch 1)

These are the "physics" of sales; they feel unnatural to product/eng/marketing people. Adopt proactively.

1. **Plenty, not scarcity.** Thousands of prospects sit behind each one; the only scarce thing is *your time*. Spend "good time with good opportunities" (Brad Snider). Ruthlessly kill marginal deals so golden ones aren't buried.
2. **Activity above all.** Activity in ≈ value out. "Jump first, prepare midair." Template everything, one proofread, send, move on. Default state = activity; its absence should feel aberrant.
3. **Be direct.** You have license to ask bluntly: do you have this pain, does it need solving, will you spend money. Asking for the sale is not optional.
4. **Many shallow relationships.** Dozens to 100+ concurrent conversations. Impossible to hold in your head → CRM discipline.
5. **Assume the sale is inevitable.** Frame "when," not "if." Consultative, confident, sets up the next funnel pass.
6. **Expect to win, be unfazed by rejection.** 20–30% win rate is *solid* for a new solution. Rejection = "not now," not "never." Record the loss reason, move on.
7. **Record everything, efficiently.** "Set future-you up for success." Log salient points so you can resurrect deals in 60–90 days.
8. **Be expert & authoritative → fearless.** Be a "student of the game," ideally more expert than the buyer. Kills the urge to over-prep.
9. **Live in a glass house.** Instrument/record all customer-facing activity. Transparency → accountability, shared learning, and removes fear of failure (the biggest brake on activity).
10. **Sales is math.** Win rate, ACV, emails/demo, opps/close, cost-of-sales. Get cozy with CRM reporting + pivot tables.

They compound: record everything → be non-scarcity & direct → higher activity → more data → better decisions.

## PART 2 — Baking the narrative + pricing (Ch 2)

**The narrative exists before and independent of any collateral.** Slides, emails, web copy, videos, demo scripts are just *implementations* of it. No coherent narrative = "shiny nonsense." It's a living hypothesis that meets the market and gets revised.

### Problem-solution-specifics framing (inductive, each part builds on the last)
1. **What is the problem?** One crisp sentence so a buyer can self-qualify.
2. **Who has it?** The person with *functional responsibility + budget*. If they can say "not my job," wrong person. Target by title. Note "complementary decision-makers" (their internal customer, e.g., the CEO who wants "ship more software").
3. **What does it cost?** Hard ROI (direct $), soft ROI (opportunity cost), directional. Scale by prospect size. *We have this: bad hire $90K, vacancy tax $24K/mo, CTO-time tax $225K/yr, ~$555K/yr total.*
4. **How is it solved now, and why does that fail?** Four incumbent levels: (a) no solution, (b) process, (c) service providers (recruiting agencies at 20–30% of first-year salary), (d) products (HackerRank/CodeSignal, LinkedIn, manual CTO interviews). Credibly acknowledge their *strengths* too.
5. **What changed → why now?** For us: LLM/voice AI finally good enough to run real technical interviews at scale, plus proctoring to catch AI-assisted cheating (a *new* problem the same shift created).
6. **How does it work?** Explain by analogy to something they know.
7. **How do you know it's better? (quant + qual)** Same metrics incumbents are judged on, plus third-party validation (customer logos, testimonials with metrics, case studies, press/analyst coverage).

> Test the whole thing as a cocktail-party elevator pitch to someone who knows the space. Then write it out end-to-end (Kazanjy does full worked versions for TalentBin, Salesforce, Groupon, HubSpot, Zendesk).

### Pricing (the conclusion of the narrative arc)
- **Charge from day one.** Free = not taken seriously, not used, no signal. Founders duck pricing to avoid "no"; that avoidance is the mistake.
- **Give more value than you capture, early.** Priced-to-perfection hurts close rates *and* churn (all stars must align for felt ROI).
- **Iterate up.** TalentBin: $99 → $199 → $299 → $399 → $499/mo, then $6,000/yr/seat. Raise until it drags close rates. **Grandfather** early customers; "buy now, lock this rate" is an inducement.
- **Three approaches:** (a) **existing-solution comps** — safest; don't innovate on product *and* pricing at once; TalentBin priced *just under* LinkedIn Recruiter's ~$8K → "smokes it and cheaper? I'll try it." (b) **ROI/value pricing** — peg to provable value (HIRABL priced for a confident 10x). (c) **value alignment + thresholding** — usage tiers, and price under a natural buying threshold (individual card / manager expense / discretionary dept budget) so they can "just try it."
- **Don't segment early.** Resist the 3-tier "Most Popular" page. You don't yet know which features matter to which segment, and you should be talking to one ICP.

## PART 3 — Sales materials + full template library (Ch 3)

**Speed over polish.** Shorter loop from hearing an objection → shipping a slide that kills it = better. Ten rough 5-min screencasts (ums and all) beat one pristine explainer. **Flash without a valid narrative is worse than nothing.** Never let "not flashy enough" block creating a slide/video.

### Slides
- **Primary asset**, even for technical founders itching to jump to the demo. Slides are visual, sendable, remixable, speak-over-able, and what B2B buyers expect. "A good presentation tees up a great demo."
- **Structure for extensibility.** MVP = one slide per narrative step. Then "zoom in / zoom out": break a value-prop slide into sub-chapters, then per-feature slides (screenshot + subtitle + supporting metric). Keep summary versions.
- **Master deck.** One big master (`Roundz_Sales_Master.pptx`); fork sales-ready / press / analyst / investor cuts. Never destroy old slides. Use ClearSlide/DocSend to send an abridged deck *and* see who viewed the pricing slide / forwarded it.
- **Section-specific notes:** open with **problem** (lets them self-disqualify early). Have a **"why we're legit"/logos** slide ("you'll be in good company"). Have a **"why this will be easy"** slide (buyers fear implementation pain, not just cost). Have a **pricing** slide (present only the option you want to push). Put edge cases + competitor comparisons in an **appendix** you flip to on demand — add an appendix slide any time a question is asked twice.
- **Production shortcuts:** reuse the fundraising deck's theme/logo/colors; drop shadows on screenshots; Camtasia GIFs as mini-demos; hire an Upwork designer to polish *after* you built it. Publicity/logo rights: bake into the MSA, ask forgiveness not permission early.

### Email templates (cold outreach) — principles
- Medium-specific encapsulations of the narrative; end goal = a demo. Split the narrative across a **multi-week drip** (short attention-grab first, then one bucket per email).
- **100% text**, no high-sheen logos/HTML (that reads as robotic spam). Plainspoken, peer-to-peer, no jargon. Prospect's POV always ("prospects don't care about you, they care about them").
- Customized subject lines carrying qualification info. **"Click targets"** (YouTube demo thumbnail, screenshot, site link) so instrumentation reveals interest. Strong CTA to a one-on-one demo.

**Verbatim example (TalentBin, short):**
```
SUBJECT: Hey {First_Name}! A magical solution to help with the pain of engineering hiring.
Hey {First_Name}, I hope you're having a great day! It's {Rep} at TalentBin, reaching out because
I have something that can make hiring all those Ruby, iOS, and Java roles I see on your career page
waaaaay easier. In short, we make technical recruiting software that:
1. Surfaces 3x as many technical candidates as a LinkedIn recruiter search.
2. Gives you personal email addresses so candidates actually read your outreach.
3. Automates the annoying repetitive parts (dozens of outbound emails in the time it takes to send one InMail).
I'd love to show you how we make you a technical recruiting superhero. Do you have 20 minutes next week?
Reply here, or ring me at {Rep_Phone}. Thanks, {Rep}
```
(Longer variants add: pain empathy → "you're frustrated by poor LinkedIn profiles, no InMail responses, too much time"; social proof → "Facebook, Amazon, Microsoft… use it daily"; a YouTube explainer link; a link to *their* careers page as a click target.)

**Warm intro variant:** don't ask the introducer to intro you directly (target should opt in). Send the introducer an easy-to-forward, pre-written email carrying your pitch + a "why you'll be excited" line + a CTA for the target to reply to *you*. Instrument it (Yesware pixel) to see when it's forwarded/opened; set a reminder to follow up with the target directly afterward, treating them as a cold target with warm context.

### Phone / voicemail scripts
- Not a word-for-word script; bullet guideposts to hit your points in 30–90 sec. A reformatting of the narrative.
- **Gatekeeper / cold-call opener (HIRABL, verbatim):** *"This is {Name} at HIRABL. We help staffing agencies identify backdoor hires. Are you familiar with backdoor hires? … Last year we found over 4,200 missed fees across just 120 customers. I'd love to set 20 minutes with our Account Director {Name} — {Day} or {Day}?"* Note: a single big proof stat + a fast CTA to a specialist demo.
- **Reaction permutations (build these):** interested → book demo off their live calendar; follow-up question → "great question, that's what the demo's for," drive to demo; "not interested" → deflect + articulate value ("I wouldn't be calling if I didn't think this helps you hire more, faster, cheaper; this is going to be industry standard"); "does it cost money?" → "not free, strong ROI," drive to demo; "is this like {Competitor}?" → "we're the original/richest/most automated, which is why a demo will complete your picture," drive to demo; hard no → "sounds like timing's off; I'm convinced this helps, so I'll send video examples and touch base in a month" (pitching-the-inevitable).
- **Voicemail = audio email.** Short, personalized, note you're the founder, always **paired with a simultaneous email** so they can just hit reply. "Customer-proof VM": lead with logos ("Facebook, Amazon, Groupon use us, so we're legit").

### Demo scripts
- Follows the deck's framing; walk the prospect's **natural workflow** step by step, starting with the most important/compelling use case (you never know when a demo ends early). TalentBin's buckets: **Search → Qualify → Reach Out → Automate.**
- **Customize with the prospect's own context** ("I saw you're hiring iOS devs in Philadelphia, let's search for those") — never generic ("Java devs in SF" when they're neither). Best case: run the demo on the prospect's *real data* (HIRABL has the prospect send submission data a week ahead, then shows the actual missed fees found: "we found ~25 missed fees worth ~$400K — want access to this instance to start collecting?"). Build product features that make customized demos easy — they raise close rates even if they add no post-sale value.
- You're doing it right when prospects say "that's awesome" / "you have no idea how much this will help with XYZ."

### Video
- **MVP overview video:** record a shortened pitch+demo on your laptop while narrating (Camtasia/Snagit/QuickTime). Goal = sell the *next step* (a demo), not the product. Production value doesn't matter ("ums" are fine if the pain is real). **Post to YouTube** (mobile-friendly, trusted URL, SEO rank, easy to share/embed); title/describe/tag it well and link back to your lead-capture form.
- **Explainer video** (animated, <2 min, higher production) is the more advanced cousin for the website — valuable but a project; don't let it gate you. Ship rough video first.

### Other collateral / one-off requests
- Treat collateral as "features": weigh build cost against use frequency × value. For one-off asks ("got a one-pager? success stories?"), first ask the "question under the question" — usually existing collateral (deck slides, a video) already answers it.
- **On-demand customized collateral is powerful and cheap:** a 60-sec screenshot ("here are your iOS-dev search results vs LinkedIn — 4x!") or a 5-min Jing walk-through hyper-specific to the prospect, shareable across their whole team.

## PART 4 — ICP + prospecting (Ch 4)

**#1 founder mistake:** selling to who's *available* (incubator mates, friends, ex-colleagues) instead of who *has the pain*. Selling to the unqualified = "setting revenue on fire" (won't close; if they do, they churn, soak up support, mislead your roadmap, badmouth you). **Qualify the account, not the relationship.** The window-repairman test: a stranger who can fix my broken window is welcome; a close friend with no broken window is not a commercial conversation.

- **Boil the ICP into metadata**, not prose. TalentBin: "5 technical recruiters, 20 open eng reqs incl iOS/Java/Android, uses LinkedIn Recruiter." Immediately: "uses Gmail + Salesforce + Marketo, 50 outside reps selling $50K software, has a Sales Ops Manager." **For Roundz AI:** Series A–C, 50–250 staff, N open engineering reqs, active technical hiring, CTO/VP Eng present, currently using HackerRank/CodeSignal/manual CTO interviews/agencies.
- **Demand signifiers** = outward proxies for pain + magnitude + ability to pay. Roll them into a **customer attractiveness score**. Include the minimum-viable bar (e.g. TalentBin: ≥1 recruiter AND ≥3 open eng roles).
- **Sources:** LinkedIn (best for titles/headcount, incl. Recruiter Lite Boolean title search + faceting by company size/geo); Datanyze/BuiltWith/Wappalyzer/Datafox/SimilarWeb (tech stack on their site); job boards / careers pages / Glassdoor / WANTED Analytics / Indeed (hiring signals — **our leading demand indicator**: open eng reqs); Yelp/Google Maps/InfoUSA for SMB; competitors' public indexes as source lists.
- **Hidden characteristics** (e.g. "do they pay for a passive-recruiting tool") → surface via discovery questions or attract via inbound later. Use correlated public signals as proxies.
- **Build the list manually to start.** Avoid bought lists (stale, sparse, poorly targeted). Manual work teaches you what the ICP really is and lets you capture rich metadata for personalized mail-merge. Don't source across many tools at once — that's "prospecting ADHD."
- **Rabbits / deer / elephants:** hunt **deer** (mid-market): enough pain to act, enough process to adopt, fast decisions, no procurement/legal drag. Elephants = slow, entrench you as a services shop, can crush you. Rabbits = tiny deals that churn. Aim for the biggest deer.
- **Geography:** start local (same time zone, on-site demos).
- **Point-of-contact discovery:** target the budget-holding, functionally-responsible decision-maker. **Cascade** titles (grab VP + Director + Manager). Consider *complementary* decision-makers (buyer's internal customer) and *bottom-up* (users → champion → budget). Look for warm-intro "ins" via shared connections — *after* qualifying the account, never as the basis for choosing it. Log "Potential Intros."
- **Contact info:** email first (enables templating, drip, open/click tracking, progress tracking). Find via search, email-pattern formation + Rapportive/FullContact/Lusha/LeadIQ validation. Capture switchboard numbers too; phone-first only for SMB where email is hard.

**Target: a hand-built list of 50–100 ICP-fit local accounts, each with contacts + demand metadata**, structured in a Google Sheet (fields you'll query or mail-merge on). Good metadata → outreach like: *"Hi {First_Name}! We can help {Company} with that {Star_Average} Glassdoor average… here's a bad review: {Review_Link}…"*

## PART 5 — Outreach & appointment setting (Ch 5)

Goal of this stage is **not** to sell the product — it's to sell a *conversation* (the demo). First-date logic: aim for the second date, not marriage. As a founder you can frame it as "research / customer development" to lower friction.

### Email
- One email won't do it. Split the narrative into short, single-thought emails and **drip** them. Calm, conversational, text, CEO-to-CEO tone.
- **Instrument everything** (Yesware/Tout/Sidekick, or Outreach/Salesloft/PersistIQ/MixMax for drip). Opens/clicks/re-opens/forwards (new geos) = buying signals → time your next call/email to them.
- **Personalization data (Outreach):** unpersonalized ~5% reply; heavily human-personalized first email ~12%; **human-personalized first email leveraged across a 7-email drip ≈ 15x the responses of one-and-done, ~30% more than heavy auto-personalization, ~50% more than a lightly customized first email.** Trick: invest ~5 min on a killer, data-rich first email ("single-serving drip campaign"); the drip replies to the same thread, dragging them back to that first email each time (leverages the 5 min at every step; replies also open at higher rates).
- **Cadence data:** the **2nd email out-performs the 1st (~18% vs ~12%)**; reply rates hold through ~5, then decline. Add a **breakup email** ("I won't email again, but I'm convinced this is relevant") to force a decision.
- **Timing:** avoid Monday-AM triage. Aim ~10am Tue, or 6–7am (phone, first thing), or after-hours 7–8pm. Add **social context** (shared connections, alma mater, recent tweet).
- **"Spam" rebuttal:** it's only spam if you targeted badly or wrote badly. Well-prospected + relevant + value-adding = welcomed.

### Calling
- **Timing** drives connect rate: early AM / end of day for office workers; **call after 6–7pm to skip gatekeepers** and reach execs; mid-day is dead. SMB timing differs (don't call a restaurant mid-service). Follow "the 10am hour" across time zones.
- **Context-sensitive:** call whoever just opened/clicked; call *immediately* on an open for big connect lifts.
- **Gatekeepers:** be pleasant (you differentiate by kindness). Give a 1-line hero-making value pitch. Ask for the direct line "in case we get disconnected." Prefer pass-to-voicemail or getting an email address over a "live message" (game of telephone).
- **Voicemail = audio email**, always paired with a simultaneous email.
- **On live connect** (~30 sec): sell the *appointment*, not the product. On "yes," schedule *right then* off their live calendar (never "I'll email times"). Book **2–3 days out, never >1 week** (attendance craters). Grab email + direct/mobile phone; consider pulling in other stakeholders/users. **Hot transfer** ("do it now"): treat as a "demo lite" for discovery + a pitch for a fuller, better-prepared demo.

### Appointment-setting objections (all variants of "not now")
- **"Call me later / next quarter"** → trade: "Happy to. Give me 30 sec now to check it's even relevant, so I don't bug you later if it's not."
- **"No budget"** → hear "not for *that*." "No problem, I just want to understand how you do [problem]; if we're relevant we can talk money later."
- **"Just send info"** → "Happy to, but a personalized version takes me 30 min and I won't do that if it's not relevant — give me 30 sec." Then pivot to selling the demo as the best way to get the info.
- On a **hard rejection**: don't argue. Frame inevitability ("I'll be back"), give an out, send a templated "rejected call" follow-up (many change their tune).

Cadence = email + call + VM woven over a sequence. Also do **referral prospecting** off happy contacts.

## PART 6 — Early inbound capture & response (Ch 6)

Don't build a content/inbound machine yet (nobody Googles a category they don't know exists). But you need a **minimum viable inbound path** because good outreach drives site visits ("outbound inbound" — the best inbound leads, because they're already qualified).

- **Big obvious "Request Demo" CTA** on the homepage (following the user down the page) → structured form (Typeform/Wufoo/Salesforce Web-to-Lead). Works on mobile.
- **Minimal fields:** first/last name, company, title, email, phone, + only the demand signifiers you *can't* find yourself. Every extra field loses leads. **Put a phone number on the form** + a checked voicemail (verbal buyers — eng leaders, recruiters, sales — will call). Chat (Drift/Intercom "mad-libs" flow) is a modern alternative if staffed.
- **Speed is everything.** MIT Sloan/InsideSales: contact rates drop **100x** from 5→30 min, **10x** from 5→10 min. Wire new-lead notifications (with phone) so you can call from anywhere; auto-responder + "call this Google Voice number now" fallback.
- **Persistence:** each attempt adds ~15% contact chance; ~6 attempts ≈ 90% cumulative, then stop.
- **Qualify the account, not the lead.** Inbound has intent but the requester may be unqualified — or an unqualified contact at a *qualified* org (pivot to loop in the right people). Funnel true-unqualified away gracefully (great experience → they refer qualified friends). Use a **"hard qualifier" email** to let likely-unqualified leads disqualify themselves while handing them shareable marketing copy.

## PART 7 — Pitching: discovery, presentation, demo, ask, objections (Ch 7)

**Persuasion formula:** `Potential Value × Value Comprehension × Belief = Likelihood/Magnitude of sale`. Maximize each: target high-pain accounts (value), present clearly (comprehension), prove it (belief: proof points, real-data demos, pilots). Buyers apply a *risk discount* to promised value; your job is to shrink it.

- **Sell in person / on-site early**, even 1–2 meetings/day. Higher fidelity, more insight, more trust. Go inside/telepresence at scale later. Batch travel; use the first face-to-face to build trust, then go digital.
- **Pre-call planning is not optional** (demo hold rates ~80%, unlike cold calls). Block 15 min before every demo (and travel-then-prep so you're 15 min early on-site). Walk in with: **pain points + "size of prize"** (how much you could sell), **competitive/complementary products + capacity to pay** (recent funding = money to execute), **potential users**, **stakeholders/influencers**, **customization data** (their reqs/careers page/Glassdoor), **icebreakers** (LinkedIn/Twitter — never politics/religion), and a written **"known unknowns"** list for discovery. Set a **stated goal**. Get "in the zone"; take the call **standing**.

### Pitch format (consistent): pleasantries → discovery → slides → live demo → proof points → pricing/ask
- **Discovery** (first 5–10 min; the thing first-timers botch worst). Frameworks: BANT / ANUM / ChAMP — but for early innovative products lean on **pain + urgency** (buyers can't have a budget/timeline for a category they didn't know existed). Use info you already found to ask **leading** questions ("Based on LinkedIn you've got reps in 4 cities — do they, like most outside teams, log poorly in the CRM?"). **Cascade down the pain** (size, current solution, its downsides — this mirrors your narrative). Tie each question to a feature you built. Cover team/authority/commercials (who buys, have they bought before, budget cycle vs discretionary). **Disqualify gracefully** if no pain/urgency/ability to pay ("I'd rather give you back 30 minutes than waste your time").
- **Presentation** sets the mental model (pain, why incumbents fail, how you work, proof) — better on slides than demo. Don't dump features. Hold feature/proof slides until after the demo.
- **Demo** proves the promises, ideally on the **prospect's real data** (Postini: "shall I draw up a proposal, or would you like your spam back?"). Else use pristine **demo-ready data** — never "ignore that / imagine if." Focus only on features that matter to *this* buyer and *this* persona.
- **Proof points** (switch back to slides): ROI studies, aggregated + per-customer ROI, testimonials. "This is why it's a no-brainer." Don't skip before asking.
- **Speak the three languages** (Skip Miller): **end users** care about easier jobs / looking good / career; **first-line managers** care about ROI, budget, team efficiency, adoption; **CXOs** care about top-line, valuation, cash.

### In-pitch techniques
- **Repetition** is your friend (new to them, old to you). Keep reconnecting slides/features to your core points.
- **Validate attention + understanding** by asking questions, not "does that make sense?" (invites fake "uh-huh"). Give permission not to know ("it's ok if you haven't, these are dorky niche sites"). Ask specific leading questions that double as agreement-building.
- **Rolling discovery:** sprinkle discovery-style questions throughout; cite their earlier answers back ("as you said earlier…").
- **Build agreement** at every step so the close is a formality.
- **Pace slowly, pause often** (critical on phone).
- **Customize + curate:** drop whole slides/features that won't resonate; double down on what will.
- **Micro-contracts** (Sandler "up-front contracts"): before each step, state what you'll do and why, get agreement; before ending, **contract the next meeting onto the calendar now**. Broken micro-contracts are your early-warning system ("we agreed X, but two meetings got canceled — is this still a priority?").

### Pricing + asking
- Founders' biggest close-stage error: **they don't ask.** If you built agreement, buyers often ask pricing themselves (a buying signal). If not, run a **trial close** ("is this something you see solving your [problem]?"). A "no" here (before price) is a red flag → dig into objections.
- Present pricing **framed vs comps / ROI / opportunity cost** ("$30/rep/mo = 15 min of salary"). Start at the **most extreme/list case**, present a **single unit then roll up**, then **shut up** and let silence work. Build in negotiation room; don't negotiate against yourself.
- **Options/proposals:** present ~3 tiers with the top as a "gold-plated" anchor that's a per-unit steal; land tiers just under psychological thresholds ($10K/$19.5K/$24K). The proposal is *marketing collateral* — it sells for you and lets your champion do show-and-tell to the CFO. Don't over-worry cannibalization early; take the cash. Deliver via templated PPT → e-sign (Conga/Drawloop/Octiv later).

### Objections (Ch 7, cont.)
Objections = engagement (good). "Respectful contentiousness" (The Challenger Sale). Force specificity: **"What specifically is blocking us from progressing right now?"**

| Objection | Handle |
|---|---|
| **No authority** | Confirm it's the *only* blocker, then *you* project-manage the next meeting with the real decision-maker (don't hand the champion the ball — advocating isn't their day job and they're worse at it). Bring strong ROI slides for the back-checker. |
| **No need** | Most concerning. Re-verify with all signifiers stated ("you have 50 outside reps who struggle to log activity — not true?"). Specific contradiction = take seriously (fix discovery next time). Squishy = cover for the real objection: "you can be straight with me." |
| **Happy as-is / fear of change** | Make the hidden status-quo cost *visible* with hard math ("you're wasting $15–40K/yr — half a coordinator's salary — your CEO would love that back"). Add FOMO ("competitors are adopting this; be the one who looks brilliant early"). Pure fear → "here are all the resources we'll put in to make you successful." |
| **Bad timing / higher priorities** | Reduce perceived effort ("say yes and it's done; we run the training"). Or reframe why it's higher priority (make the *other* project's ROI case and win it). Or sleight of hand: lock it now with an inducement (14 months for 12, price rising, start next month). |
| **Price/value** | Diagnose: discount-posturing (~10% below → just negotiate) vs genuine value gap (~50% below → walk ROI/comps piece by piece: "where's my analysis wrong?"). A legit gap can reveal a pricing-model insight (they use only part of the product, different best-alternative). Early on, get the deal done at a discount if true-ICP — but **document the rationale in the contract** so it's not "standard" and can be removed at renewal. Don't sell to someone who genuinely gets minimal value. |
| **No budget** | Usually failure of imagination. Find budget: (a) reallocate from the problem's existing spend (agency fees, incumbent-tool renewals), (b) cross-bucket transfer (payroll → tooling: "Zendesk lets you hire 3 agents instead of 5 → $100K freed"), (c) discretionary/experiment budget (small land-and-expand). Loop in the CFO (thinks big-picture). Only disqualify if the org has *no* concept of spending to make money. |

## PART 8 — Down-funnel: negotiation, closing, pipeline (Ch 8)

### Negotiation
- Expect it from experienced buyers. **Inflate the opening** so you end up viable; leave room for procurement's second bite.
- **Levers:** price, amount, contract **duration**, **payment terms**, auto-renew/opt-outs. **Cash up front is king** for early-stage (AR risk), then contract length (reduces churn risk).
- **Retreat one increment at a time**; when they ask for something, move a *different* lever your way (want per-seat discount? buy more seats / longer term). Often they just want to *feel* they got a deal — a quick 5% closes it and frees you for the next deal.
- **Tactics:** cheaper per-unit → buy more; cheaper total → fewer seats + higher per-unit, or shorter term; shorter duration → 2–3x per-time price (onboarding is a fixed cost); split payments → raise price (don't finance customers). **Urgency:** rest-of-month free, limited CS onboarding slots, price-going-up, lock-rate-now. **BATNA:** status-quo alternative → charge for the delta; real competitor → out-product them and charge for it (document why their "extra features" are overkill), or price to real value. Make them *prove* a competitor's quote and commit to sign same-day before you match (avoid a reverse auction). Use an "authority backstop" ("let me check with my VP") to push back on discounts.

### Closing (winning)
- Move fast once they say yes — no happy dance. **Order form ≠ contract.** Keep the order form dead simple (terms + signature); host the legal **MSA** separately and link to it (don't staple legalese to the order form — it invites questions and stalls). Always **e-sign** (HelloSign/Adobe eSign/DocuSign). Redlines: try "our paper or no-go," cave if needed, watch legal-fee bleed (another reason to sell deer). 
- **Get paid.** FreshBooks + card/PayPal for speed; prefer paid-up-front annual over recurring billing now; cash > AR headaches.
- **Warm handoff to CS** — capture decision-maker/sponsor/user names + contacts at close.

### Closing (losing) — normal
- 10–20% win rate typical for a new solution; 30% is great; if you win everything, your pipe is too thin or too cherry-picked. **Close lost decisively** rather than let zombies clog the pipe (your time is the asset). Give a direct offramp ("we've missed two follow-ups — still interested, bad timing, or not a fit? I can take the truth"). Never burn bridges. **Record closed-lost metadata** (reason, competitor picklist, notes) — a treasure map for future-you + roadmap input. **Loop back in ~60 days** with a *new* opportunity ("we shipped a ton, you're still hiring engineers — want to catch up?"). Until they lose the need or buy a competitor, they're a target.

### Pipeline management
- **Stages** with crisp, consistent definitions (TalentBin: Demo Scheduled → Qualified → Seeking Approval → Sent Proposal → Negotiation → Verbal → Contract Sent → Closed Won/Lost/Unqualified). Ignore Salesforce's generic stages/close-percentages.
- **Every open opp needs an explicit next step with a calendared date.** Pipelines die from missing next actions.
- **Cadence:** block dedicated pipeline-review time (TalentBin: 2 hrs Wed PM). Learn your "carrying load" (max opps before close rates drop) and cap new demos.
- **Work close to the money:** review bottom-of-funnel first (contracts, verbals, proposals), prioritized by value × ease. Then fix mid-funnel opps with no calendared next action. **Clean ruthlessly** — a stalled mid-funnel opp squats on room for a juicier one. Reports: rank by stage×value; error-check down-funnel opps with no activity in 7 days or stuck >2 weeks or with no future meeting ("uncovered opps").
- **Calendar by role/time:** AM = prospecting/outreach; mid-day/early-PM = demos; end of day = follow-up/wrap. Mon/Fri have high demo-cancel rates → double up on prospecting/pipeline then.
- **Role specialization = first scaling lever:** an **SDR** ($40–60K) feeding 5–10 demos/week onto the founder/AE calendar pays for itself fast (a demo at 25% × $5K ACV = $1,250 of expected revenue). Math: $50K SDR + $120K AE ≈ $650K/yr at ~26% cost-of-sales; 1 SDR : 2 AEs ≈ $1.3M at ~22%. Starts the virtuous cycle (SDRs → AEs → pull in friends as SDRs). Get an SDR wingman as soon as you can reliably set your own appointments.

## PART 9 — Customer success as a marketing engine (Ch 9)

CS is growth/marketing, not a cost center. The moment you close, a countdown to renewal starts.

- **The math:** 5% monthly churn roughly halves revenue vs 0% over ~58 months ($4M → <$2M MRR); **2.5% monthly *expansion* takes the same business to $7M+ MRR.** Land-and-expand + word of mouth is the whole game. Even more important early: happy first customers lower the leap-of-faith for the next prospect ("they told them about us over a beer").
- **CS = delivering the exact ROI promised in the sales narrative**, per stakeholder. Our KPIs (faster screening, less CTO time, fraud caught, dollars saved) are the CS scorecard. Implementation is step one — get them to a fast first win; make sure pricing covers CS labor (if implementation is 120 min and a $50K CS rep does 15/week, that's ~$70/customer — a $20/mo product can't support it).
- **The virtuous cycle to engineer:** fast win → they double the contract → take reference calls → record a webcam testimonial for the deck → cite you at a conference → their departing user's first call is to buy for their new company. Do CS wrong and it all inverts (churn, cancellation, badmouthing).

## PART 10 — Scaling maturity model + early sales management (Ch 10)

**When to scale:** win rate reliably **15–30%** at healthy ACV + deal cycle. Above → raise price AND scale; below → fix messaging/product/pricing first. Target **cost-of-sales 20–30% max** (a $100K full-cycle rep should book ~$500K; at 20% win × 5 demos/wk × $10K ACV ≈ $480K; at 10% win ≈ $240K = 40% cost-of-sales, unscalable).

**Anti-patterns:** *premature scaling* (Zenefits, Square Stand 2014 — hire reps before proving founder-led sale → burn + churn) and *lagged scaling* (founder keeps turning the crank instead of "packaging" the motion → opportunity cost). Specialization is worth it when efficiency gains beat coordination overhead (true for higher-ACV, longer-cycle sales; not for low-ACV one-call closes).

### The maturity model — each stage has a goal + exit criteria
1. **Founder does it all** (lead gen → close → CS). *Exit:* a handful of paying customers who use it, get value, and would renew/refer. *Anti-pattern:* "hire a sales guy to figure it out," not charging, or selling without ensuring value.
2. **Founder + SDR** (or **Founder + CSM** if lead-gen is easy but CS is the bottleneck). First specialization; frees founder for more selling/CS. *Exit:* a few dozen customers acquired + onboarded to success, with a clear, documented, repeatable sales motion and healthy KPIs. *Anti-pattern:* handing the "prove it's repeatable" job to a hired sales guy / VP Sales; spray-and-pray.
3. **Founder + SDR + 2 AEs.** Goal: prove *someone other than the founder* can sell it. Founder shifts toward training/coaching/inspection/tooling. *Exit:* those AEs closing ICP customers who get to success, at least as efficiently as the founder did. *Anti-pattern (goldilocks):* too many reps too fast ("boil the ocean," none reach critical mass) OR founder still "playing" instead of "coaching."
4. **Initial "Sales Pod"** (founder steps *entirely* out of day-to-day selling). Prove a complete unit (lead gen + selling/closing + onboarding) produces revenue predictably with smooth handoffs, good unit economics, and high-NPS onboarding. Founder now does orchestration, process definition, tooling. *Exit:* predictable revenue, all members hit KPIs, unit throws off cash — "you're confident you can clone this."
5. **Pod abstraction & initial scale** (clone the pod). More managerial/metrics complexity; maybe add a professional sales manager (or prove another cohort or two first). *Exit:* multiple units producing predictably, returning lots of contribution margin — confident a professional manager could run and clone them.
6. **Full scaled team-of-teams** (Ch 8 role specialization) — and you know it works because you did it yourself.

**Sales ops** ("product management of the sales org" — remove friction/bugs, add features): worth a dedicated head once ~10 reps (e.g. an $80K sales-ops person lifting five AE/SDR pods by $50K each = $250K incremental). Until then it's everyone's job.

### Early sales management (the mindset flip)
- The high-order change: **you no longer *do* sales; you help others sell.** Any time you're doing individual sales work (a demo, a prospect email), ask if a *manager* activity should replace it. Even if you close 30% at $50K vs reps' 20% at $30K, your incremental ~$80K/mo is nothing vs adding four reps who each pump $50K/mo. **Tend the "pipeline above the pipeline" — the hiring pipeline.** (Classic failure: "seasoned" managers from Oracle/IBM/SAP who sell alongside reps instead of hiring/coaching.)
- **Manager activities (do only high-leverage things — "recurring impact, makes >1 person better"):** build systems/playbooks/processes/materials; hire + onboard (rigorously — the old "hire 10, wash out 5" is wasteful; filter in hiring, ramp with hands-on reps); construct + document the process ("Rules of Engagement" for SDR→AE→CS handoffs); monitor adherence + coach; select/administer tooling; build + monitor the **metrics harness** (quantity × quality of customer-facing activity). Protect calendar time for management or it won't happen.
- **The driver of GTM success:** *high quantity of high-quality customer-facing selling activity.* Raise quantity (focus, specialization/automation, more reps) and quality (training, messaging, process). "Adding cylinders to the engine, hooked up right, burning cleanly."

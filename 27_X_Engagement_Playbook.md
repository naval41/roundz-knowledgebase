> Researched live on 2026-09-11 via the logged-in @roundz_ai session. Six searches, ~200 posts scraped, filtered to 40 relevant, top threads below.
> Same non-negotiables as the Reddit playbook: disclose, one product mention max, the reply must be useful with the mention deleted, never fabricate.

## PART 0: What the search showed

1. **The category is crowded and noisy.** "AI interviewer" search is dominated by vendors posting into the void (HelloRecruiter/ARIA at 4 views, Heizen, Elly, Micro1/ZARA, Conveo just raised $50M Series A, HackerRank launched "Chakra" AI interviewer on Jan 30). Posting "we built an AI interviewer" gets zero engagement. Do not do that.
2. **What actually gets engagement is the pain, not the product:** cheating in interviews (22 to 38% of candidates using AI live), "coding interviews in 2026 are absurd", "interviews that ban AI test an outdated way of working", founders burning 4 hrs/day screening. These are the threads to live in.
3. **Nobody in the results is arguing our exact angle** (test how well an engineer *directs* AI, instead of banning AI). HackerRank's Chakra pitch is "conversational, not checklist". Micro1 is "AI phone screen". The agentic-coding round is an open lane on X.
4. **Candidate-side content is hostile to AI interviewers** (Justine Moore's "hallucinating back at the AI interviewer" got 24.7k likes; "cracked the Micro1 interview" threads get 1.7k likes). Engage with empathy there, never defend the category.

## PART 2: Reply drafts

Written for a founder account. If posting from @roundz_ai, keep the same text; the handle itself is the disclosure. No link in the first reply. Link only if someone asks.

**#1 @kelvinbuildss ("should we introduce AI to tech interviews?")**
> Yes, and make it the test. Give the candidate a copilot and score how they direct it: does the prompt carry the constraints, do they verify the output or ship it blind, do they catch the wrong abstraction on the second turn. That signal is invisible in a no-AI round and it's most of the job now. (We run this as its own round at Roundz, still learning what the rubric should weigh.)

**#2 @jeremybernier ("coding interviews in 2026 are absurd")**
> Agree on all three, and the fix isn't harder questions. If every question leaks in a day, stop scoring the answer and score the process: how someone reasons through complexity, what they verify, how they steer an AI when it's wrong. Leaked questions don't help with that because the thing being measured is judgment, not recall.

**#3 @samposwal (200 applicants, 4 hrs/day)**
> Been there. The part that hurt most for us was that the first 15 minutes of every call was the same: are they real, can they reason about their own resume, do they clear the bar. That's the slice that's automatable. Keep the human hours for the top 20% where the call actually decides something. Disclosure: building exactly this (roundz.ai), so biased, but the 80/20 split held for us before we built anything.

**#4 @rohanpaul_ai (cheating, deepfakes, hand-waving)**
> The hand-wave is proof the model is upside down. If the interview is "answer this without AI", every incentive points at hiding AI. Flip it: the interview is "here's an AI, solve this with it, we're watching how you drive". Cheating stops being a concept because using the tool is the assessment. Identity checks still matter, but they stop carrying the whole load.

**#5 @SundarRKrishnan ("AI exposed the cracks")**
> Exactly. The crack it exposed: we were testing recall (algorithms, syntax) because it was cheap to grade, not because it predicted the job. Now recall is free, so the only thing left worth testing is judgment and how someone works with the tools. Curious what you're seeing recruiters replace LeetCode screens with.

**#6 @rvivek (HackerRank Chakra launch)** (never bash a competitor)
> Congrats on the launch. "Follow a checklist instead of actually interviewing" is the right diagnosis. The other gap I keep seeing across AI interviewers (ours included, until recently): none of them measured whether the engineer can direct an agent. Conversational is table stakes now; agentic-coding assessment is where the signal is going.

**#7 @randomrecruiter ("interviewing is a different skill than the job")**
> Which is an indictment of the interview, not the engineer. The closer the round is to the actual job (their real stack, an AI copilot they'd actually use, a system-design question from your real backlog), the smaller that gap gets. Interview-as-a-skill exists because we made the format artificial.

**#8 @OwenGregorian (HBR "AI has broken hiring")**
> The HBR framing misses that AI broke the *cheap-to-grade* part of hiring: resumes and canned answers. That was never the signal, it was the proxy. What's left is expensive-to-grade signal (live reasoning, tool use, design tradeoffs), and the interesting question is whether AI can grade that explainably so humans only see the top slice.

**Stock reply for the recurring "22% / 38% cheat with AI" stat posts**
> The stat only alarms you if the interview bans AI. Flip the round so using AI is the task and that number becomes "38% of candidates already have the skill you're hiring for".

**Candidate-side threads (#10 Micro1, Justine Moore "hallucinating back")** (do not pitch; if you reply at all)
> Fair. A checklist AI interviewer deserves to be gamed. The test of a good one is whether it notices when you hallucinate back at it.

## PART 3: Rules for this account

- **Cadence:** 3 to 5 value replies per day, at most 1 disclosed product mention per 10 replies (same 10% rule as Reddit).
- **Timing:** reply within 30 to 60 minutes of a post from accounts with 5k+ followers. Old threads earn history, not reach.
- **Format:** quote-post with commentary beats a plain reply for reach. Plain reply beats a quote-post for relationship. Use quote-posts on #2, #4, #8; plain replies on #1, #3, #5, #7.
- **Never:** argue, defend AI interviewers to candidates, post "we built X" cold, drop links unasked, use identical text across threads (X's spam filter catches duplicate replies).
- **Follow-up:** follow every author above and turn on notifications for @jeremybernier, @rohanpaul_ai, @randomrecruiter, @rvivek, @samposwal. Their next post is a fresh thread we can be first on.

That sounds like a role I know well. After a decade in the trenches sourcing engineers, I’ve seen the hiring landscape shift dramatically, especially in the small-to-mid-cap space (SMEs).

Unlike Big Tech (FAANG), where the machine is well-oiled (even if slow), small to mid-sized companies often struggle with an identity crisis in their hiring process. They want top-tier talent but often lack the infrastructure to evaluate or attract it effectively.

Here is the "inside track" on the major systemic failures I see in hiring today for this segment:

1. The "Unicorn" Job Description
This is the most common sin. A startup or mid-sized firm needs a Senior Backend Engineer. But when you look at the JD, they list:

Expertise in Distributed Systems

DevOps/Kubernetes mastery

Frontend (React/Angular) proficiency

"Bonus points" for GenAI experience

The Problem: They are trying to hire an entire IT department in one person. This scares away specialists who don't want to be "jacks of all trades," and it attracts generalists who may not have the depth required for the core architectural work.

2. The "LeetCode" Disconnect
Small companies often copy the interview tactics of Google or Amazon (hiring committees, intense algorithmic puzzles) without having the same use case.

The Reality: If you are a Series B startup, you need someone who can ship clean code and fix a production fire today, not someone who can reverse a binary tree on a whiteboard but can't configure a CI/CD pipeline.

The Friction: We lose great pragmatic engineers because they fail an academic puzzle that has zero relevance to the day-to-day CRUD API work the company actually does.

3. The "Ghost" in the Machine (Process Chaos)
In mid-sized companies, the "Hiring Manager" is often also the CTO or a Lead Dev who is already working 60 hours a week.

The Consequence: Resume feedback loops take weeks. Candidates are left in limbo. By the time the company decides to move forward, the good engineers have already accepted offers from companies that moved faster. Speed is the only advantage small companies have over Big Tech, and they frequently squander it.

4. "Culture Fit" as a Mask for Bias
Smaller teams are protective of their culture ("We work hard, play hard," "We are a family").

The Issue: This often translates to hiring people exactly like themselves. It creates a homogeneous engineering culture that lacks diversity of thought. It also often masks a lack of work-life balance; they filter out candidates who ask about boundaries or process, labeling them as "not hungry enough."

5. Equity vs. Cash Delusion
Many mid-level founders overvalue their equity. They offer lower base salaries with the promise of "life-changing" stock options.

The Shift: After the market corrections of recent years, senior engineers are cynical about paper money. They want cash liquidity. Companies that refuse to adjust their cash component to market rates (expecting candidates to take a risk on the equity) are consistently losing the battle for senior talent.

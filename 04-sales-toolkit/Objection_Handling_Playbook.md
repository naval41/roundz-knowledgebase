# Roundz: Objection Handling Playbook

**For:** Founder-led and early sales conversations with CTOs, VPs Eng, and Talent leaders.

**Method (Kazanjy loop): use this on every objection:**
1. **Acknowledge**: validate the concern; never argue.
2. **Clarify root cause**: ask a question to find what's *really* behind it.
3. **Reframe / evidence**: reposition around the real issue with honest proof.
4. **Confirm**: check you've resolved it ("Does that address the concern?").
5. **Advance**: propose the concrete next step.

**Honesty guardrails (never break):**
- Never claim we execute code or report test-case pass rates. We **evaluate approach, complexity, and correctness of reasoning.**
- Cost/fraud stats are **industry research**: always attribute (US DoL/SHRM; Officevibe/LinkedIn; Stripe Developer Coefficient; Glider AI). Never present as Roundz outcomes.
- Never invent logos, testimonials, or customer results.
- Human-in-the-loop always: Roundz filters; the employer meets the top ~20% and makes the final call.

## 1. Trust & Quality

### "AI can't judge candidates like my staff engineer can."
- **Underlying concern:** Fear of losing nuanced human judgment; skepticism that a model has taste.
- **Response:**
  > "Totally fair, and we agree, which is why Roundz never makes the hire. **Can I ask: what does your staff engineer look for that you're worried we'd miss?** Roundz isn't replacing that judgment, it's doing the first-pass filtering your senior people don't have time to do consistently across every candidate. It probes reasoning live over voice, watches the code and design as they build, and scores against a level-aware rubric, then your staff engineer meets only the pre-vetted top ~20% and makes the call. You get *more* of your best engineer's judgment on the candidates who actually deserve it, not less."
- **Confirm:** "If your senior engineers spent their interview time only on a pre-vetted shortlist, would that be a win?"
- **Advance:** Offer a pilot where their staff engineer reviews Roundz reports side-by-side with their own read on the same candidates.

### "Why should I trust the score?"
- **Underlying concern:** Black-box AI; can't defend a decision internally or legally.
- **Response:**
  > "You shouldn't trust a black box, so we don't give you one. Every report is **explainable and level-aware**: it's scored against an ENTRY/MID/SENIOR rubric with a verdict from STRONG_HIRE to STRONG_NO_HIRE, and it shows the evidence behind each dimension, the reasoning the candidate gave, how they approached the design, how they directed the AI copilot. You can read *why*, disagree, and overrule it. It's a decision-support tool, not a verdict machine."
- **Confirm:** "Is an explainable, evidence-backed report something you could stand behind with your team?"
- **Advance:** Walk through a real sample report in the demo.

### "Does it actually run and grade my code?"
- **Underlying concern:** Wants to know the evaluation is rigorous and real, not superficial.
- **Response (guardrail-critical):**
  > "Good question, and I want to be precise: Roundz **does not execute code or report test-case pass rates**: that's deliberately not the signal we sell, because 'the code runs' is exactly the signal AI copilots broke. Instead we evaluate the things that actually predict a good engineer: **approach, complexity, and the correctness of their reasoning**: watched live in the Monaco editor and the Excalidraw design board, with the voice interviewer probing trade-offs as they go. We measure how they think, not whether a test suite went green."
- **Confirm:** "Given that 'does it compile' no longer separates candidates, does reasoning-level evaluation match what you actually want to measure?"
- **Advance:** Show the live code + design awareness in the demo.

### "AI will be biased."
- **Underlying concern:** Legal/ethical risk; fairness; adverse-impact exposure.
- **Response:**
  > "It's the right thing to worry about with any hiring tool, including human panels, which carry their own well-documented bias. Two things: first, Roundz scores against a **consistent, structured, level-aware rubric applied the same way to every candidate**, which is more standardized than ad-hoc human interviews. Second, **a human always makes the final decision**: we filter, you decide, so there's a human check in the loop by design. We're glad to walk your team or counsel through exactly what's evaluated and how."
- **Confirm:** "Would it help to review the evaluation dimensions with your team before a pilot?"
- **Advance:** Offer a rubric walkthrough; loop in their legal/People contact if useful.

## 2. Candidate Experience

### "Candidates will hate a bot interview."
- **Underlying concern:** Drop-off, brand damage, cold/impersonal experience.
- **Response:**
  > "That's the fear with most automated screens, a static, one-way test. Roundz is different: it's a **live voice conversation** that adapts and asks follow-ups, so candidates get to actually explain their thinking instead of being filtered by a keyword. And it's on **their** schedule, no waiting a week for a slot. Most candidates find 'show me how you reason' far more respectful than a take-home that vanishes into a void. The ones who reach your team arrive already engaged."
- **Confirm:** "If the experience felt like a real conversation candidates could do anytime, would that ease the concern?"
- **Advance:** Offer to let them *take* a Roundz interview themselves in the demo.

### "Our employer brand will suffer."
- **Underlying concern:** Public perception; Glassdoor; competitive talent market.
- **Response:**
  > "Brand is exactly why the *quality* of the experience matters. Two things protect it: candidates get a fair, level-aware read and a real conversation instead of a black-hole take-home, and because you're only meeting the vetted top ~20%, **your team can give those finalists a genuinely high-touch human experience** instead of rushing 20 rushed screens. Better use of everyone's time usually *helps* brand."
- **Confirm:** "Does concentrating your team's human attention on finalists sound like a brand upgrade or downgrade?"
- **Advance:** Pilot on one req and gather candidate feedback directly.

### "Senior candidates won't do it."
- **Underlying concern:** Top talent has options and won't jump through hoops.
- **Response:**
  > "Agreed, senior people won't tolerate busywork. But the Roundz round isn't busywork; it's a **substantive technical conversation plus an agentic-coding round that respects how they actually work**: solving *with* an AI copilot, which is what their real job looks like now. Strong seniors tend to *prefer* being evaluated on judgment and how they direct AI over yet another whiteboard trivia grind. And it's short and on their schedule."
- **Confirm:** "If the round felt like real senior work rather than a hazing ritual, would your seniors engage?"
- **Advance:** Show the agentic-coding round; suggest piloting on a senior req.

## 3. Need / Status Quo

### "We already use HackerRank / CodeSignal / Karat."
- **Underlying concern:** Already spending; switching cost; "good enough."
- **Response:**
  > "Those are solid at what they were built for, and most of them assume the pre-AI world where 'can the candidate produce working code alone' was the signal. That signal degraded once copilots made code run for almost anyone. Roundz adds the layer they don't: a **live voice reasoning interview**, awareness of both code *and* system design as it happens, and the **agentic-coding round that measures how well someone directs AI**: the actual job now. Plenty of teams run us *alongside* an existing tool on one req to compare the signal before changing anything."
- **Confirm:** "Would a side-by-side on one req be a fair way to see if the added signal is real?"
- **Advance:** One-req pilot running parallel to their current tool.

### "Our recruiters / team handle this fine."
- **Underlying concern:** No perceived pain; protecting team/turf; change aversion.
- **Response:**
  > "That's great, and Roundz isn't here to replace them, it's here to give them their time back. **How many engineer-hours go into first-round technical screens each month?** Roundz takes the top-of-funnel filtering off their plate so recruiters and engineers spend their time on the vetted top ~20%. Industry research (Stripe's Developer Coefficient) consistently shows engineer time is the scarcest, most expensive resource, this points more of it at finalists and offers."
- **Confirm:** "If your team kept the decision but stopped doing first-pass screens, would that free up meaningful time?"
- **Advance:** Quantify their current screening hours; pilot to measure hours saved.

### "We don't hire enough to need this."
- **Underlying concern:** Low volume; ROI doesn't pencil out.
- **Response:**
  > "Makes sense to question fit at low volume. The flip side: when you hire rarely, **each hire matters more and a mis-hire hurts more**: industry research (US DoL/SHRM) pegs a bad hire at up to ~30% of first-year salary. Roundz isn't priced or built only for high volume; even on a single critical req it de-risks the most important decision. It's about signal quality on the hires you *do* make, not just throughput."
- **Confirm:** "For a role you can't afford to get wrong, is better signal worth a look?"
- **Advance:** Pilot on their single most important open req.

## 4. Fear of Change / Risk

### "What about data privacy, security, and PII?"
- **Underlying concern:** Compliance, candidate data handling, security review burden.
- **Response:**
  > "Right to raise it early. We're glad to walk your security team through our data handling, retention, and access controls, and to answer a security questionnaire. Candidate data is handled for the purpose of the evaluation with appropriate controls, and a human always makes the final decision. **Who on your side would need to sign off, and what's their process?**"
- **Confirm:** "Can we get your security contact what they need to green-light a pilot?"
- **Advance:** Introduce to their security/People contact; provide data-handling documentation.
- *(Note: state only what is accurate; if a specific certification isn't in place, say so and describe current controls rather than overclaiming.)*

### "What if the AI fails mid-interview?"
- **Underlying concern:** Reliability; candidate stranded; brand risk.
- **Response:**
  > "Fair, a candidate stuck mid-interview is exactly the failure mode we design against. The platform is built to handle interruptions gracefully and let candidates resume, and because there's a **human reviewing every shortlist**, no single automated moment decides someone's fate. If anything looks off in a session, it surfaces to a human rather than silently failing."
- **Confirm:** "Would seeing how a session recovers and how humans stay in the loop address the reliability worry?"
- **Advance:** Demonstrate the flow; agree on a support path for the pilot.

### "How does it integrate with our ATS?"
- **Underlying concern:** Workflow friction; won't adopt if it lives outside their stack.
- **Response:**
  > "Good, adoption dies when a tool lives in a silo. **Which ATS are you on, and where in your funnel would Roundz sit?** For a pilot, teams often start with a lightweight flow, send candidates in, get reports back, before wiring up deeper integration, so you can prove the signal without a big IT project up front. Then we fit it to your workflow."
- **Confirm:** "Could we start with a low-lift flow for the pilot and formalize integration once the signal proves out?"
- **Advance:** Identify their ATS and design the pilot flow around it.

## 5. Authority / Budget / Timing

### "I need to check with my team / legal."
- **Underlying concern:** Not the sole decision-maker; needs buy-in or cover.
- **Response:**
  > "Absolutely, this should be a team decision. **Who else needs to weigh in, and what would they each need to see to feel good about it?** I can put together exactly what your engineers, People team, and legal would want, the evaluation rubric, a sample report, and data-handling details, so you're not the one fielding all their questions."
- **Confirm:** "If I package that up, could we get the right people in a 30-minute session?"
- **Advance:** Secure a group demo with the stakeholders named.

### "No budget right now."
- **Underlying concern:** Real budget constraint, or a soft brush-off, or ROI unproven.
- **Response (re-anchor on ROI: never lead with a discount):**
  > "Understood, let's make sure it's worth budgeting at all before we talk dollars. The cost we'd be offsetting is real: senior-engineer interview hours (Stripe Developer Coefficient shows that's your priciest resource) and the downside of a mis-hire (US DoL/SHRM: up to ~30% of first-year salary). The **one-req pilot is designed to prove the ROI on a single role first**, so any spend is anchored to a result you've already seen, not a leap of faith."
- **Confirm:** "If a pilot showed clear time savings and better signal on one req, would that help you make the budget case?"
- **Advance:** Scope a right-sized one-req pilot; align on the metric that would justify budget.

### "Let's revisit next quarter."
- **Underlying concern:** Not urgent; deprioritized; polite defer.
- **Response:**
  > "Happy to time it to your hiring. **Are you hiring engineers this quarter, though?** If so, every screen you run in the meantime is spending your engineers' time on the old signal, the one AI copilots already eroded. A single-req pilot now costs little and means that when next quarter's hiring ramps, you already know whether this works for you instead of starting the evaluation cold."
- **Confirm:** "Would running one quiet pilot this quarter make next quarter's decision easier?"
- **Advance:** Book a small pilot now; set a firm check-in date next quarter.

### "Just send me some info." (brush-off)
- **Underlying concern:** Disengaging politely; not enough value established to warrant time.
- **Response:**
  > "I'll send something, and to make it useful rather than another PDF in your inbox, **what's the one thing you'd most want it to answer?** Is it how we evaluate without running code, the candidate experience, or the ROI? I'll tailor it to that and include a 3-minute sample report so you can judge the actual output, not just marketing."
- **Confirm:** "If I send exactly that, can we grab 20 minutes next week to react to it?"
- **Advance:** Send a targeted one-pager + sample report with a specific follow-up booked.

## 6. Competition: Differentiate Without Trash-Talking

**Rule:** Respect the incumbents; win on the wedge. Never disparage HackerRank, CodeSignal, Karat, or Final Round: position them as good tools for a world that changed.

- **The reframe:** "Most screening tools were built to answer 'can this person produce working code on their own?' AI copilots made that question easy to fake, so the signal moved."
- **The wedge (three pillars):**
  1. **Agentic-coding round**: the only round that scores **how a candidate directs an AI copilot** (prompt quality, decomposition, judgment, token efficiency). This is the actual AI-native job, and it's what nobody else measures.
  2. **Live voice reasoning + design awareness**: a real adaptive conversation that watches code *and* system design as they happen and probes trade-offs, not a static, async test.
  3. **Explainable, level-aware reports**: ENTRY/MID/SENIOR rubric scoring with a verdict and the evidence behind it, so decisions are defensible.
- **Scripted line:**
  > "Tools like [X] are genuinely good at what they were built for. The gap we fill is the AI-native part of the job: we measure how someone *reasons out loud* and how well they *direct an AI copilot*, and we hand you an explainable, level-aware report, with your team making every final call. Easiest way to see the difference is to run us on one req next to whatever you use today."
- **Advance:** Side-by-side pilot on one req.

## 7. Pricing

**Rule:** Re-anchor on ROI and outcomes. Never lead with a discount, it signals the price was inflated and trains the buyer to push.

- **Underlying concern:** Value vs. cost; comparison shopping; internal justification.
- **Response:**
  > "Let's anchor price to what it's worth to you rather than a sticker. Two costs it offsets, from industry research: your senior engineers' interview hours (Stripe Developer Coefficient, your scarcest resource) and the cost of a mis-hire (US DoL/SHRM, up to ~30% of first-year salary). The pilot is **priced against a single requisition** so you see the signal and the time savings on one role before scaling. When the pilot shows the ROI, the pricing conversation is easy because it's grounded in a result you've already seen."
- **If pushed for a discount:**
  > "I'd rather prove value than cut price, if the pilot doesn't show you real signal, discounting it wouldn't make it worth your team's time. Let's make the pilot small enough that the risk is low and the proof is real."
- **Confirm:** "If the pilot proved the ROI on one req, would the pricing feel justified?"
- **Advance:** Scope the one-req pilot and define the success metric up front.

## Objection Prevention: Address the Top Ones Pre-emptively

Handle the predictable objections *before* they're raised, in the deck and demo, so the conversation stays on value:

1. **"Does it run my code?"** → Say it plainly up front: *"We don't execute code or report pass rates, we evaluate approach, complexity, and correctness of reasoning."* Kills the biggest misframe early.
2. **"AI replaces my judgment."** → Lead the demo with the **80/20 human-in-the-loop** model: Roundz filters, your team meets the top ~20% and decides. Make it a headline, not a footnote.
3. **"Candidates will hate it."** → Let the buyer *take* the interview in the demo. Experiencing the live voice conversation dissolves the "cold bot" fear faster than any slide.
4. **"How is this different from what I use?"** → Put the **agentic-coding round** front and center as the wedge, it's the one thing no incumbent measures.
5. **"Trust the score?"** → Always show a **real sample report** (explainable, level-aware, with evidence) in the demo, not just a number.
6. **"Bias / privacy / reliability."** → Proactively state the human-in-the-loop safeguard and offer a security/data-handling walkthrough before they have to ask.
7. **Cost stats** → Whenever you cite the cost of a bad hire or engineer time, **say the source out loud** (US DoL/SHRM; Stripe; Officevibe/LinkedIn; Glider AI) and label it industry research, pre-empts "is that your data?"

**Close every conversation the same way:** propose the **one-req priced pilot** as the low-risk way to convert any remaining doubt into evidence.

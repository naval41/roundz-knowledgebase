# Pilot Offer + Pricing Strategy

**Audience:** Employer / CTO / VP Engineering / Head of Talent (B2B buying motion)
**Purpose:** Give the founder a repeatable way to sell a *paid, de-risked* first engagement, anchor price on ROI, and convert pilots into recurring credit-pack / subscription revenue.
**Product:** Roundz (roundz.ai), AI technical-interview platform.

> **How to read the numbers in this doc.** Every specific dollar and credit figure below is a **FOUNDER-VALIDATED PLACEHOLDER**: a starting point to test against real willingness-to-pay, not a committed price. They are marked `[PLACEHOLDER]`. Validate with the first 5–10 conversations, then lock.
>
> **Honesty guardrails (do not violate in any customer-facing use of this doc):**
> - Roundz **evaluates approach, complexity, and correctness of reasoning** on coding work. It does **not** claim to execute code or report pass-rates.
> - All cost/time statistics are **industry research** (US DoL / SHRM; Officevibe / LinkedIn; Stripe Developer Coefficient; Glider AI), **not** measured Roundz outcomes.
> - No fabricated customers, logos, or case studies. Where proof is missing today, the pilot exists to *create* it (see "What to instrument").

## 0. Narrative spine (how this doc is organized)

**Problem → Cost → Why Now → Solution → Proof → Ask.**

| Beat | One-line version to say out loud |
|---|---|
| **Problem** | Your senior engineers burn hours screening candidates, and a lot of that time is spent on people who never should have reached them. |
| **Cost** | Interviewing is expensive senior-engineer time; a bad hire is far more expensive; slow loops lose good candidates. (Industry research.) |
| **Why now** | Candidates now interview *with* AI copilots. Old screens either ban that (unrealistic) or can't tell who's actually driving. |
| **Solution** | Roundz runs your real loop with a live voice interviewer, live code + whiteboard awareness, and an agentic-coding round that scores how a candidate *directs* an AI, then hands you explainable, level-aware reports. Human-in-the-loop: you meet the top ~20% and make the final call. |
| **Proof** | Explainable rubric reports you can audit line-by-line, run side-by-side against your current screen during the pilot. |
| **Ask** | Run one open role through a paid, de-risked pilot with success criteria we agree on up front. |

## 1. The Pilot Offer: "Start With One Req"

The flagship entry motion. Small, fast, one requisition, clear success criteria, but **priced**. A paid pilot proves the value exchange is real; free-forever pilots train buyers to expect $0 and rarely convert.

### 1.1 Scope

| Dimension | Pilot definition |
|---|---|
| **Roles** | **1 open requisition** (pick a role the customer is actively hiring for and has candidate flow on). |
| **Volume** | Screen the **next N candidates** for that req: suggested **N = 20–30 candidate-interviews** `[PLACEHOLDER]`. Enough signal to compare, small enough to stay low-risk. |
| **Rounds** | The customer's real loop, configured to level (ENTRY / MID / SENIOR): live voice interview + live code/whiteboard + the agentic-coding round. |
| **Duration** | **2–4 weeks** from kickoff (driven by their candidate flow, not by us). |
| **Human-in-the-loop** | Roundz filters; customer's team meets the **top ~20%** Roundz surfaces and makes every final call. Roundz never "rejects" for them. |

### 1.2 What the customer gives

- One active req + JD, and their target level(s).
- Access to the candidate flow for that req (or a shared outreach plan) so we can hit N.
- ~60–90 min of one engineer's time to calibrate the rubric to their bar at kickoff.
- A named point of contact and agreement to a 30-min readout at the end.
- **Payment up front** for the pilot pack (see pricing).

### 1.3 What the customer gets

- Level-aware, **explainable** rubric reports on every screened candidate, with a verdict on the `STRONG_HIRE → STRONG_NO_HIRE` scale and the reasoning behind it.
- Reports on the **top ~20% within the week** they interview, fast enough to act on live pipeline.
- A **side-by-side comparison** vs their current screen (where they choose to run both): where Roundz and their process agreed, and where they disagreed and why.
- Proctoring signals and semantic resume→JD match context on each candidate.
- A joint end-of-pilot readout measured against the success criteria below.

### 1.4 Success criteria: agreed up front (mandatory)

A pilot without written success criteria is a demo, not a pilot. Fill these in *with* the buyer at kickoff, in writing. Suggested defaults:

| # | Success criterion | Suggested target `[PLACEHOLDER]` |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Senior-engineer screening time saved** on this req | ≥ 50% reduction in hours their engineers spend on first-round screens |
| 2 | **Time-to-shortlist** | Top ~20% surfaced within the week candidates interview |
| 3 | **Decision agreement** | Hiring manager agrees with Roundz's verdict on the shortlist in ≥ 80% of cases |
| 4 | **Report trust** | HM rates the explainability/usefulness of reports ≥ 4/5 |
| 5 | **Candidate experience** | No material increase in candidate drop-off vs current screen |

> Only criteria the customer can actually observe in 2–4 weeks. Do **not** promise retention or "quality of hire" outcomes inside a pilot window, those take quarters and would breach the honesty guardrails.

### 1.5 Conversion path (pilot → recurring)

```
Qualify one req  →  Sign paid pilot (cash up front)  →  Kickoff + calibrate rubric
   →  Screen N candidates  →  Weekly shortlists  →  End-of-pilot readout vs criteria
      →  IF criteria met:  convert to Growth credit pack OR Annual subscription
      →  IF partially met:  scope a second req / extend, re-set criteria
      →  IF not met:        close-lost with reason capture (see §5)
```

The readout meeting *is* the close. Walk in with the results mapped against the agreed criteria and a pre-filled proposal for the next step.

### 1.6 Exact ask language (use verbatim / lightly adapt)

**The pilot ask:**

> "Here's what I'd propose: pick one role you're hiring for right now. We'll run your next ~20–30 candidates through Roundz: your real loop, calibrated to your bar with one of your engineers at kickoff. Within the week each candidate interviews, you get explainable reports on the top ~20%, and you can run it side-by-side against your current screen so you're comparing apples to apples. We agree on the success criteria up front, the main one being at least a 50% cut in the senior-engineer hours you spend screening for that role.
>
> The pilot is **$[X]** `[PLACEHOLDER]`, paid up front, and it covers those ~20–30 interviews. At the end we sit down for 30 minutes against those criteria. If we hit them, we roll into a credit pack or an annual plan and I'll credit the pilot toward it. If we don't, you've spent two to four weeks and $[X] to find out, no ongoing commitment. Which role should we point it at?"

**The credit-back close (trade, don't give: see §4):**

> "I'll apply the full pilot fee as credit toward your first annual plan if we start within 30 days of the readout."

## 2. Value-based pricing logic

Price off **value delivered**, not cost-plus. The buyer isn't paying for "interviews run"; they're paying for **reclaimed senior-engineer time, faster time-to-hire, and bad-hire avoidance.** Anchor every price conversation there.

### 2.1 The three value levers

1. **Senior-engineer interview hours saved**: the most concrete and defensible. Hours no longer spent on first-round screens × loaded hourly cost of the engineer.
2. **Faster time-to-hire**: shorter loops reduce candidate loss and the carrying cost of an open role (lost productivity of the unfilled seat).
3. **Bad-hire avoidance**: better-calibrated, explainable screening reduces the probability of a costly mis-hire. (Use as *upside*, framed conservatively, never as a guaranteed Roundz outcome.)

### 2.2 ROI formula (screening-time lever: the one to lead with)

```
Annual screening hours saved  = (first-round screens/yr) × (eng-hours per screen) × (% of that time Roundz removes)

Annual $ value (time)         = Annual screening hours saved × loaded hourly cost of engineer

Time-to-hire value            = (days saved per hire) × (daily carrying cost of open role) × (hires/yr)

Bad-hire avoidance (upside)   = (bad-hire rate reduction) × (fully-loaded cost of a bad hire) × (hires/yr)

Total annual value            = Time value + Time-to-hire value + Bad-hire avoidance

ROI                           = (Total annual value − Roundz annual cost) / Roundz annual cost
```

### 2.3 Worked example: ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY

> **Illustrative.** Inputs are round numbers + **industry-research** cost assumptions (US DoL / SHRM loaded-cost conventions; Stripe *Developer Coefficient* for engineer-time waste; SHRM cost-of-vacancy / cost-of-bad-hire framing). These are **not** measured Roundz results. Replace every input with the customer's own numbers on the call.

**Assumptions (illustrative):**

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| First-round technical screens per year | 300 |
| Senior-engineer hours per screen (incl. prep + debrief) | 1.5 hrs |
| Share of that first-round time Roundz removes | 60% |
| Loaded hourly cost of a senior engineer | $120/hr |
| Hires per year from this pipeline | 20 |
| Days of time-to-hire saved per hire | 7 |
| Daily carrying cost of an open senior role | $500/day |
| Fully-loaded cost of one bad senior hire (industry research) | $150,000 |
| Bad-hire rate reduction (conservative) | 1 avoided every 2 years → 0.5/yr |

**Calculation:**

- Screening hours saved = 300 × 1.5 × 60% = **270 hrs/yr**
- Time value = 270 × $120 = **$32,400/yr**
- Time-to-hire value = 7 × $500 × 20 = **$70,000/yr**
- Bad-hire avoidance (upside) = 0.5 × $150,000 = **$75,000/yr**
- **Total annual value ≈ $177,400/yr** (or **$102,400/yr** if you exclude the bad-hire upside and lead only with hard time savings)

Against an illustrative Roundz annual cost of **$24,000 `[PLACEHOLDER]`**, that's roughly a **4x ROI on hard time savings alone**, before the bad-hire upside. **Lead with the conservative number** ($102K) and present bad-hire avoidance as additional upside, it keeps you inside the honesty guardrails.

## 4. Terms & negotiation

### 4.1 Terms to default to

- **Cash up front.** Pilot is prepaid. Annual is billed annually up front, not monthly, wherever the buyer will accept it (via Razorpay).
- **Annual with auto-renew.** Default annual contracts to **auto-renew** with a standard notice window (e.g. 30 days before term end) `[PLACEHOLDER]`. State it plainly in the order form, no dark patterns, just a default.
- **Pilot credit-back.** Pilot fee converts to credit toward an annual plan **only if** they commit within a defined window (e.g. 30 days of readout). This rewards momentum and protects the "paid pilot" principle.

### 4.2 Negotiate on terms, not just price: trade-don't-give menu

Never discount for free. Every concession you give should buy something back (term, volume, cash, or proof).

| If the buyer wants… | Ask for in return |
|---|---|
| A lower per-credit / annual price | **Longer term** (2-year commit) or **larger up-front credit commitment** |
| To start smaller / cheaper | **Multi-req commitment** or a signed **expansion trigger** (auto-upgrade at usage threshold) |
| A discount | **Case-study rights** (named, with metrics from the instrumented pilot) |
| A bigger discount | **Logo rights** + a **reference call** for future prospects |
| Monthly billing | A **rate premium** vs annual-up-front, or a shorter path back to annual at renewal |
| Extra seats / more integrations | A **longer term** or a **higher tier** commit |

### 4.3 Handling "it's too expensive"

Do **not** immediately cut price. Re-anchor on ROI:

1. **Reframe to value, not cost:** "Compared to what? A typical alternative here is your senior engineers' hours, let's put real numbers on that." Walk the §2 formula with *their* inputs.
2. **Shrink the unit:** break annual cost down to **cost per candidate-interview** and set it against a single senior-engineer screening hour.
3. **Shrink the risk, not the price:** if they still balk, hold price and offer the **de-risked pilot**: smaller commitment, same rate, prove it first.
4. **Trade to close the gap:** if you must move on price, take a concession from §4.2 (term, logo, case study, larger commit). Price only moves when something comes back.
5. **Never race to $0.** A free-forever deal violates the value-exchange principle and poisons the reference.

## 5. Close-lost handling

Dead deals are data and future revenue. Never let one go dark.

### 5.1 Capture WHY (structured, every time)

Log a single reason code + a one-line note the moment a deal is lost:

| Reason code | Meaning | Reopen trigger |
|---|---|---|
| `NO_BUDGET` | No budget this cycle | New fiscal period / new funding |
| `NO_HIRING_NOW` | Hiring frozen / no active reqs | Reqs reopen |
| `PRICE` | Value not yet believed at this price | New ROI proof / case study |
| `TRUST_AI` | Skeptical of AI-led screening | New proof, explainability demo |
| `COMPETITOR` | Chose/kept another tool | Their renewal date |
| `TIMING` | Right fit, wrong moment | Stated future date |
| `NO_DECISION` | Stalled, no champion | New champion / org change |

### 5.2 Nurture cadence

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| **Day 0** | Thank them, log reason code, confirm the *specific* condition under which it makes sense to revisit. |
| **+30 days** | One useful, non-selling touch (relevant industry-research insight, product update tied to their objection). |
| **Quarterly** | Check the reopen trigger. If hiring resumed / budget reset / their competitor's renewal nears → re-approach with the pilot. |
| **On new proof** | When a fresh (real) case study or capability lands that answers their exact objection, reopen directly referencing it. |
| **Renewal date (competitor losses)** | Diarize the competitor's renewal window; re-approach ~60 days out with the pilot offer. |

Actively work the close-lost list, a "no" is usually a "not yet," and the paid pilot is the low-risk re-entry every time.

## 6. What to instrument during the pilot

The first pilots exist to **manufacture the proof the rest of the sales motion currently lacks.** Instrument every pilot from day one, and get sign-off to use the results (traded per §4.2). Capture (with the customer's own baseline where possible):

- **Senior-eng screening hours before vs. after**: the headline ROI number. Get their per-screen baseline at kickoff.
- **Time-to-shortlist**: hours/days from candidate interview to shortlist delivered.
- **Decision agreement rate**: % of shortlisted candidates where the hiring manager's judgment matched Roundz's verdict (and where it didn't, why).
- **Verdict distribution**: spread across `STRONG_HIRE → STRONG_NO_HIRE` for the cohort (context for calibration, not a quality claim).
- **Side-by-side deltas**: where Roundz and their existing screen agreed/disagreed on the same candidates.
- **Candidate experience**: completion rate, drop-off, and (if collected) candidate satisfaction / NPS.
- **Agentic-coding-round signal**: qualitative notes on how well "ability to direct an AI copilot" differentiated candidates (a genuinely novel data point, capture verbatim HM reactions).
- **Report-trust rating**: HM's 1–5 rating of explainability/usefulness, plus quotes.
- **Credits consumed vs. planned**: from the audit ledger; informs pack sizing and unit economics.
- **Champion quotes + logo/case-study consent**: capture in the readout while enthusiasm is highest.

> Every metric above must be labeled as *this customer's pilot result*, never generalized into a product-wide claim, and never conflated with the industry-research cost stats used to size ROI. That discipline is what keeps the proof credible.

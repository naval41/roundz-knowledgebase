# Cold Outreach Cadence + Demand-Signified ICP: Roundz (roundz.ai)

**Motion:** Employer / CTO B2B outbound
**Wedge:** The "agentic-coding" round, the first interview that measures how well an engineer *directs* AI.
**Method:** Kazanjy, *Founding Sales* (demand-signified metadata, Cold Calling 2.0, multi-touch cadence)

---

## 0. The one-liner you are selling on every touch

> **Your screening tests if the code runs. Roundz tests if the engineer does.**

Roundz runs your real technical loop with a live voice AI interviewer, watches the candidate's live code (Monaco) and system-design whiteboard (Excalidraw) in real time, and includes a unique **agentic-coding round** where the candidate solves *with* an AI copilot and we score *how they direct it*, prompt quality, decomposition, judgment, token efficiency. You get explainable, level-aware (ENTRY / MID / SENIOR) rubric reports with a hire verdict (STRONG_HIRE → STRONG_NO_HIRE). **Human-in-the-loop always: we filter the funnel; your team meets only the pre-vetted top ~20% and makes the final call.**

**Taglines to rotate in subject lines / openers:**
- "The AI interview engine for the AI-native era."
- "Your screening tests if code runs. We test if the engineer does."
- "Meet only the top 20%. Let AI vet the rest, and keep the final call."
- "The first interview that measures how well you direct AI."

**Honesty rails (never break these in any message):**
- Never say we "run test cases," "execute code," or quote "pass rates." Say we **evaluate approach, complexity, and correctness of reasoning.**
- Any statistic is **industry research, always labeled with a source.** Never framed as a Roundz outcome.
- No fabricated logos, customers, or testimonials.

## 1. ICP as Demand-Signified Metadata

The point of the ICP is not a vibe ("mid-size tech companies"). It's a set of **observable, searchable attributes** that (a) *signal the pain* and (b) *signal the ability to buy*. If you can't filter for it in Sales Navigator, a job board, or a funding database, it isn't in the ICP.

### 1.1 The attribute table

| # | Observable attribute | How you find it | Why it signals PAIN | Why it signals ABILITY TO BUY |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **50–250 employees, engineering-heavy** | LinkedIn headcount filter + "% in Engineering" | Enough hiring volume that screening is a real cost; not so big they've built internal tooling | Past seed-stage scrappiness; has budget lines |
| 2 | **Series A → Series C** | Crunchbase / PitchBook / Tracxin funding stage | Growth mandate = aggressive hiring plan | Fresh capital earmarked for headcount |
| 3 | **Actively hiring N+ engineers now (N ≥ 3 open eng reqs)** | Company careers page, LinkedIn Jobs, Ashby/Greenhouse boards | Open reqs = live, funded, time-boxed pain | Reqs are budgeted; someone owns the spend |
| 4 | **Remote / hybrid workforce** | Job post location field ("Remote", "Hybrid") | Remote hiring = higher fraud/impersonation risk; can't read the room in person |  |
| 5 | **Already using HackerRank / CodeSignal / Karat / Codility** | Job-post language ("complete a HackerRank"), BuiltWith/Wappalyzer, careers-page redirects | They already *believe in* structured screening, and are feeling the signal die post-AI | They already pay for a screening tool → line item exists, we replace/augment |
| 6 | **Using Greenhouse / Lever / Ashby ATS** | Careers URL patterns (`boards.greenhouse.io`, `jobs.lever.co`, `jobs.ashbyhq.com`), BuiltWith | Mature enough to instrument the funnel; cares about metrics | Has recruiting ops budget and process ownership |
| 7 | **High applicant volume per req** | LinkedIn "X applicants" counter; large public talent-community posts | Recruiters drowning; low-signal screening is eating the week | Willing to pay to compress the funnel |
| 8 | **Recent eng-leadership hire (VP Eng / Head of Talent in last ~6 mo)** | LinkedIn "changed jobs" filter | New leaders re-tool process; open to change in first 90 days | New leaders get a budget mandate |

> **The compound target:** *A Series-A-to-C, 50–250-person, engineering-heavy, remote/hybrid company running Greenhouse + HackerRank with 3+ open engineering reqs right now.* Every one of those is filterable. That's a demand-signified account.

### 1.2 Rabbits, Deer, Elephants: we hunt Deer

| Class | Profile | Deal size / cycle | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rabbits** | <50 people, seed/bootstrapped, hiring 0–2 eng/yr | Tiny ACV, no process pain, no budget | **Skip.** Too small to feel the pain or pay for a fix. |
| **Deer** 🎯 | 50–250, Series A–C, hiring 3–20+ eng, already pay for a screening tool | Meaningful ACV, weeks-not-quarters cycle, one economic buyer you can reach | **HUNT THESE.** Big enough to have the pain and a budget; small enough to buy fast without a committee. |
| **Elephants** | 1,000+, multiple procurement gates, security review, SOC2/vendor onboarding | Large ACV but 6–12 mo cycle, RFPs, incumbent lock-in | **Not now.** Will stall a founder-led motion. Revisit post-traction. |

**Focus 100% of outbound on Deer.** They convert fast enough to keep a founder-led loop alive and are large enough that a one-req pilot proves ROI.

## 2. Where to Source Target Lists

Build the list from **live buying signals**, not static firmographics. An open engineering req is the single strongest signal, it's funded, time-boxed pain.

### 2.1 LinkedIn Sales Navigator (account + lead filters)

**Account search filters:**
- **Headcount:** 51–200 and 201–500 (trim manually to ~250)
- **Headcount growth:** Engineering headcount growing >10% / 12 mo
- **Industry:** Software Development, Internet, IT Services, Financial Services (fintech), Health Tech
- **Hiring on LinkedIn:** = Yes
- **Company HQ:** your target geos (start where your network is)
- **Department headcount:** Engineering ≥ 30% of company

**Lead search filters (to find the humans inside qualified accounts):**
- **Title:** VP Engineering, CTO, Head of Engineering, Head of Talent, Director of Talent Acquisition, Engineering Manager, Head of Recruiting
- **Filter:** "Changed jobs in last 90 days" → new leaders re-tool
- **Posted on LinkedIn in past 30 days** → warm, active, reachable
- Save as a **lead list** and set alerts.

### 2.2 Job boards = the live buying signal

- **LinkedIn Jobs / Indeed / Wellfound (AngelList):** search `"Software Engineer" OR "Backend Engineer"` filtered to your headcount band. Count of open eng reqs = intensity of pain.
- **ATS boards directly:** search Google for `site:boards.greenhouse.io "software engineer"`, `site:jobs.lever.co "engineer"`, `site:jobs.ashbyhq.com`. A company with 5+ live eng reqs on Greenhouse is a Deer with a bleeding funnel.
- **Signal to log:** does the JD mention a coding assessment ("you'll complete a HackerRank / CodeSignal")? That's attribute #5 confirmed, they already pay for screening and are feeling it break.

### 2.3 Funding databases (ability-to-buy signal)

- **Crunchbase / PitchBook / Tracxn:** filter Series A–C, raised in last 12 months, headcount 50–250. Recent raise → hiring plan + budget.
- **Newsletters:** Term Sheet, Strictly VC, PitchBook Daily, new raises are pre-qualified accounts. Reach out within 2–4 weeks of a raise ("congrats on the round → now you're about to hire 20 engineers → here's how to screen them for the AI-native era").

### 2.4 Tool-stack detectors

- **BuiltWith / Wappalyzer:** detect Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby on careers pages.
- **JD language mining:** grep job posts for "HackerRank," "CodeSignal," "Karat," "Codility," "take-home." Confirms attribute #5.
- **LinkedIn "how they hire" posts / eng blog:** teams that write about their interview process are teams that care about it, high intent.

> **Output of this step:** a spreadsheet with columns: Company | HQ | Headcount | Eng % | Funding stage / last raise | # open eng reqs | Screening tool | ATS | Signal notes | Economic buyer | User | Influencer | Source.

## 3. Point-of-Contact Map (Cold Calling 2.0)

**Principle: email high, get referred down.** Don't pitch the top, ask the top *who owns this*. A referral from the CTO to their hiring manager converts far better than a cold hit on the hiring manager.

| Role | Titles | What they care about | Your angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Economic buyer** | CTO, VP Engineering, Head of Talent / TA | Eng velocity, cost of a bad hire, time leaders lose to screening, hiring bar in the AI era | Business case: reclaim leader time, raise the bar, meet only the top 20% |
| **User** | Engineering Hiring Managers, Team Leads | Their own interview panels; sick of interviewing people who passed the take-home then can't reason live | Day-to-day pain: fewer, better on-sites; explainable reports they trust |
| **Influencer** | Senior ICs / staff engineers, Technical Recruiters | Interview quality, candidate experience, funnel throughput | The agentic-coding round tests the skill their team actually uses now |

**The Cold Calling 2.0 move:** Send a short "referral email" to the economic buyer that does NOT try to sell a meeting, it asks to be pointed to the right person.

> *"Hi [CTO], is the person who owns your engineering interview loop [Name], or should I be talking to someone else on your team?"*

A one-line reply ("that's Priya, our Head of Eng") is a warm internal referral. You then open the next email with *"[CTO] suggested I reach out…"*, now you're not cold.

## 4. Qualification: ANUM checklist tuned to Roundz

Use **ANUM** (Authority, Need, Urgency, Money), better than BANT for founder-led sales because you lead with *authority* and *need*, and treat budget as a late gate.

| Letter | Question | Green flag for Roundz |
|---|---|---|
| **A: Authority** | Do they own or heavily influence the interview process / hiring tools budget? | CTO / VP Eng / Head of Talent, or a hiring manager who can pull them in |
| **N: Need** | Are candidates passing screening then failing the live round? Is leadership time being eaten by screening? Do they worry about AI-assisted cheating? | Yes to any, especially "we can't tell who can actually reason vs. who leaned on ChatGPT" |
| **U: Urgency** | Are there open eng reqs *right now*? A hiring surge post-raise? A recent bad hire? | 3+ live reqs, or "we need to hire 15 engineers this half" |
| **M: Money** | Do they already pay for a screening tool (HackerRank/CodeSignal/Karat)? | Yes → budget line exists; we're a replace/augment, not a net-new spend |

**Disqualify fast if:** <3 eng hires/year, no existing screening process, no reachable economic buyer, or an active enterprise procurement freeze. Move them to a nurture list; don't burn cadence slots.

## 5. The Multi-Touch Cadence (~2.5 weeks, multi-channel)

Assumes you already sent the optional CC2.0 referral email and got pointed to the right contact. If not, Day 1 goes to the economic buyer.

**Rules:** one CTA per touch (a 20-min call); every touch adds value or a new angle; stop the moment they reply and switch to a human conversation.

| Day | Channel | Touch | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Email | **Template A**: pain-led cold email (agentic wedge) | Land the core insight; get a reply or a "tell me more" |
| **2** | LinkedIn | **Connection request** (no pitch, Template LI-1) | Get a face next to the name; warm the channel |
| **4** | Email | **Template B**: reply-thread bump with a new angle (industry stat) | Reframe the cost of the problem |
| **6** | Phone + VM | **Cold call**; if no answer, **leave voicemail** (Section 8) | Break into a live conversation; VM primes the next email |
| **7** | Email | **"Just left you a voicemail" 1-liner** | Tie channels together; lowest-friction reply ask |
| **9** | LinkedIn | **Follow-up DM** (Template LI-2), share the 1-line proof / demo link | Value-add touch on a different surface |
| **11** | Email | **Template C**: the "different angle" email (system-design / anti-cheat) | Hit a second pain in case the first didn't land |
| **13** | Phone + VM | **Second call attempt** + short VM | Second live shot at a busy buyer |
| **15** | Email | **Template D: the break-up email** | Trigger loss-aversion; harvest the "not now, but circle back in Q_" replies |

After Day 15: move non-responders to a quarterly nurture (new-feature or new-funding trigger re-entry).

## 10. Instrumentation & Metrics

Track every touch in the CRM. You're not optimizing volume, you're optimizing the **funnel math that tells you when it's safe to scale.**

| Metric | Definition | Healthy early target |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery / bounce rate** | Emails delivered vs bounced | <5% bounce (else clean the list) |
| **Open rate** | Opened / delivered | ≥40–50% (proxy; unreliable, don't over-index) |
| **Reply rate** | Any reply / contacts touched | **8–15%** on a tight, demand-signified list |
| **Positive reply rate** | Interested replies / total replies | ≥30% of replies |
| **Meeting-set rate** | First meetings booked / contacts in cadence | **3–8%** |
| **Show rate** | Meetings held / meetings set | ≥70% |
| **First-meeting → won (pilot)** | Pilots started / first meetings held | **15–30% before you scale** |
| **Channel attribution** | Which touch/day produced the reply | Double down on what works |

**The scaling gate (Kazanjy):** Do **not** hire reps or automate volume until you personally, repeatedly hit a **15–30% first-meeting-to-won** rate on the Deer segment. That proves the message + ICP + motion are real. Until then, every non-reply is data, tighten the list or rewrite the pain line, not just the send volume.

**Weekly review loop:**
1. Which subject lines got opens/replies? Kill the losers.
2. Which *pain angle* (agentic wedge vs anti-cheat vs cost) converted? Lead with the winner.
3. Which ICP attribute correlated with positive replies? Tighten the filter toward it.
4. Where did booked meetings stall in qualification? Fix the ANUM gap.
